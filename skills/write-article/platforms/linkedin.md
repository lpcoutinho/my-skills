# LinkedIn

Short, professional-to-professional text: a recognizable problem, what the author learned or uses, and where to go next. 250 to 450 words.

## Structure

1. `# Title`: the article's promise in at most 10 words. A subtitle may follow a colon.
2. **Hook**: two or three short paragraphs. The feed cuts the text after about two lines ("see more"), so the first sentence names a pain the reader has already felt. What the article offers comes right after.
3. **Body**: two to four short `##` sections, one idea each. Use a list when the items are truly parallel (benefits, steps); otherwise, prose.
4. **Closing**: one sentence on the author's or the reader's next step, without a moral.
5. **Links**: a call-to-action line in bold, then the list of links with descriptive text. Links live here, not mid-text.
6. **Image suggestions** section (`##`): three numbered options, each with a bold name, a description and, for generated images, a prompt in parentheses. End with a bold tip saying which one to use and why. A real screenshot of a terminal, repository or tool is usually the best option.

Headings, the call-to-action line and the tip are written in the article's language.

## Watch out

- Feed posts do not render Markdown: headings, bold and links show up as raw text. If the author says it is a feed post rather than a LinkedIn article, deliver it without `#`, `**` and `[]()`, with the links spelled out at the end.
- Hashtags only if the author uses them. At most three, on the last line before the image suggestions.
